We submit the manuscript entitled “Interpretable ECG Anomaly Scoring in Walsh–Hadamard Coordinates: Orthogonal Invariance and Exact Time-Domain Score Decomposition” for consideration as an Original Research Article in Signal Processing.

The manuscript develops a methodological signal-processing framework for reference-based waveform scoring in sequency-ordered Walsh–Hadamard coordinates, using ECG windows as a structured physiological signal setting. The central question is when a transform-domain quadratic detector genuinely uses the transform and when it is only a coordinate rewrite. The paper proves that full-covariance Mahalanobis-type scoring with isotropic trace-scaled regularization is invariant under every orthonormal coordinate change. Therefore, the full-covariance Walsh–Hadamard score is exactly equivalent to the corresponding time-domain score.

The manuscript further derives an exact time-domain score decomposition by symmetric whitening and inverse orthonormal back-projection, and introduces sequency-block covariance scoring as a Walsh-specific structured extension that preserves exact decomposition while breaking full orthogonal invariance. To make the empirical implications explicit, the revised manuscript includes a competitive ablation design contrasting time-domain full covariance, Walsh full covariance, Walsh diagonal covariance, Walsh sequency-block covariance, classical transform-domain Mahalanobis scores, and generic anomaly-detection baselines. It also reports a real-data PTB-XL pilot on public ECG records. The pilot confirms the orthogonal-invariance and exact-decomposition identities at floating-point scale and transparently shows that the unoptimized nominal reference score should not be interpreted as a clinical diagnostic classifier. These additions clarify which comparisons are algebraically redundant, which ones are scientifically meaningful, and where the method's current applied limitations are.

The work is aligned with the scope of Signal Processing because it contributes to statistical signal processing, detection and estimation, spectral/signal representation analysis, structured covariance modeling, and biomedical signal processing. The ECG component is used as a structured waveform setting for methodological verification and illustration; the manuscript does not make clinical diagnostic claims.
